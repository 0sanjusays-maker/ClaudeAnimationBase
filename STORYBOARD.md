# THE LAST BENCH — storyboard and timing sheet

**50.0 s · 1200 frames · 24 fps · 1920×1080 · 16:9**

Logline: A tired teacher calls the roll on the last day of a school that closed seventeen years ago. Four names answer
him from empty desks and a textbook that writes back — and the fifth name in the register, in red, is his own.

Built film: `out/last_bench.mp4` (with sound, `assets/last_bench_audio.wav`). Scene code: `src/scenes/last_bench.js`.
Animator handoff in the original layered format: `HANDOFF.md`. Frame-accurate sound cues: `AUDIO_CUES.md`.

---

## World

The classroom is drawn flat — no projection, no 3D rotation — in one warm, dirty palette. Light comes from one warm key
and from the hallway behind the door. The colour arc runs from **amber daylight** (shot 1) through **sodium dim** (shot 9
on) to **brown-black** (shot 16), with two exceptions that are the whole film: the **blank page** (the brightest thing on
screen) and the **red ink** (the only red).

The room is already dead when the film starts: the plant in the corner is dead, the 2007 class photo is peeling, forty
desks are empty and one of them is pulled out of the row. The teacher has not noticed. The story is him noticing.

## Motifs and the rhyme

- **The pen tap** — tick-tick-tick, three. His rhythm. He taps it on the register, the podium, and in shot 7 he taps
  **four** times because nothing answers. In shot 9, four taps come back from the last bench, in his own rhythm.
- **The page that turns itself** — the book writes the boy's line three times (shots 10, 12, 15). Each time the camera
  is closer, and each time the sentence is shorter, until "par aap bhi toh…" stops mid-sentence.
- **Glasses off, rub, back on** — three times (shots 7, 11, 14). First impatience, then embarrassment, then grief.
- **The 2007 class photo** — peeling in shot 1, peels 2 mm more in shot 16, and pays off as the photograph pasted in the
  register in shot 19: the same man, twenty-five, all his hair, the same cracked glasses.
- **The ending rhymes with the opening**: shot 1 is the room with the teacher not in frame; shot 20 is the same room in
  black, with a school bell and a child's laugh, cut off mid-laugh.

## Cast

**Ravi Verma**, 42, looks 50. Round puffy face, tired eyes, mole under the left eye, thinning hair slicked back, cracked
glasses (his right lens), white kurta with a yellowed collar, ink on his right thumb. He stoops slightly, as if the room
is a little low. He never raises his voice.

**The boy** (never seen): Rahul Mishra, fourteen, dead seventeen years. He is not a ghost in the room — he is the room.
He is polite, a little annoyed, and very tired of being looked at every day and never called.

## Shots

Each shot lists its **reads**: the things the viewer has to understand, in order, with the frames each one gets. One
read at a time — a read never starts while the last one is still landing.

### 1 · 0.0–1.5 s · F001–F036 · THE ROOM
Wide, low, 35 mm, 3° Dutch. Teacher not in frame.
Static. The room is the event: forty empty desks, one pencil left in a desk, a paper crumpled on another, a bottle with
no cap, `ATTENDANCE` half-written on the board over last period's erased question, the clock, the fan, the peeling 2007
photo, the dead plant, the last bench pulled two inches out of the row with a textbook open on it.
- F001–F012 **read:** this is a school, and it is empty. (0.5 s)
- F012–F024 **read:** one desk is out of line; something is on it. (0.5 s)
- F024–F036 **hold.** Fan blades turn 15° a frame, dust drifts, the clock ticks at F024, the light drops 10% for one frame at F020.

### 2 · 1.5–3.0 s · F037–F072 · THE BENCH
One continuous push, no cut: 10%, then another 15%, then into the page until the blank paper fills the frame.
- F037–F052 **read:** we are going to the bench that is out of line.
- F052–F066 **read:** the page on it is blank. (It is the brightest thing in the room.)
- F066–F072 **hold** on blank paper. The page rustles 1 px at F055.

### 3 · 3.0–5.0 s · F073–F120 · THE TEACHER
Medium, he is left of centre at the podium, back three-quarters to us. He finishes `ATTENDANCE` on the board, turns his
head, then his body. Pen taps the podium at F088, F091, F094.
- F073–F085 **read:** there is a man here, and he is writing the roll.
- F085–F097 **read:** he turns; his face is tired, not cruel.
- F097–F120 **read:** he speaks to the room as if it were full. *"Panch log hone chahiye is class mein… count karo, kitne hain?"*

### 4 · 5.0–7.0 s · F121–F168 · THE REGISTER
Top-down on the open register: five neat names, and at the bottom, in pencil: **R.M. — seat 4, last row.**
- F121–F138 **read:** the names are written and the ink is dry; this book has not been opened in a long time.
- F138–F156 **read:** the pencil note. He is not looking at it. We are.
- F156–F168 **hold.** His thumb taps name one, tick-tick-tick (F140, F144, F148).

### 5 · 7.0–9.0 s · F169–F216 · "RAHUL?"
Medium. He looks to row one, right side. A desk with a pencil still in it answers him.
- F169–F184 **read:** he asks, and expects an answer.
- F184–F196 **read:** the beat before the answer. Pen taps twice only.
- F196–F208 **read:** the answer comes from off-screen, from nobody. *"Present, sir."*
- F208–F216 **hold.** He blinks, slow, six frames. He does not understand yet. Neither do we.

### 6 · 9.0–11.0 s · F217–F264 · "AMAN?"
Same medium, other side. The crumpled paper on that desk moves **1 mm, for two frames only** (F253–F254).
- F217–F232 **read:** second name, second empty desk.
- F232–F244 **read:** the beat; the pen taps twice.
- F244–F252 **read:** *"Present, sir."* — closer this time.
- F253–F264 **read:** the paper moves. Nothing else does.

### 7 · 11.0–13.0 s · F265–F312 · "VIKRAM"
Close on his face. This is where the film changes temperature.
- F265–F280 **read:** third name, quieter.
- F280–F296 **read:** nothing answers. Four taps instead of three, slower: he is waiting.
- F296–F312 **read:** glasses off, rub the eyes, glasses back on. He has no explanation. Hold on confusion.

### 8 · 13.0–15.0 s · F313–F360 · "KOI HAI HI NAHI"
Medium-wide. He looks down at the desks, then at the register in his hand.
- F313–F330 **read:** he checks the room, then the book, then the room.
- F330–F348 **read:** *"Lekin… yahan toh koi hai hi nahi!"* — flat, tired, not frightened.
- F349 **read:** the fan stops mid-rotation, blades frozen diagonal. **F351:** the clock's second hand stops.
- F353–F360 **hold** 0.3 s of silence. This is the first silence in the film.

### 9 · 15.0–17.0 s · F361–F408 · TAPPING
Wide, from behind him, down the room to the last bench in shadow. Light drops 20% and goes warmer.
- F361–F376 **read:** the room is darker, and he is standing in it.
- F376–F400 **read:** four taps on wood come back from the last bench, in his own rhythm. No hand. The page on the
  bench catches a little light on each tap.
- F400–F408 **hold.** He has not turned around yet, and we wait with him.

### 10 · 17.0–19.5 s · F409–F468 · "MERI ATTENDANCE REH GAYI"
Close on the textbook. The page turns by itself, and the words are already on it in pencil: **"Sir, aap mujhe har roz
dekhte ho."**
- F409–F421 **read:** blank page, the brightest thing in a dark room.
- F421–F445 **read:** the page turns itself. (One second. Let it finish.)
- F445–F468 **read:** the sentence, written on as we watch. *"Sir… meri attendance reh gayi."* — a fourteen-year-old
  boy, annoyed, not spooky.

### 11 · 19.5–23.0 s · F469–F552 · THE TURN
Locked-off medium. He turns in three parts: head, then shoulders, then feet. Then the glasses, again.
- F469–F492 **read:** the head turns first. (Neck creak.)
- F492–F516 **read:** the shoulders follow; the kurta rustles.
- F516–F540 **read:** the feet; the shoes scrape on the floor.
- F541–F552 **read:** glasses off, rub, on. *"Tum kaun ho?"* Quiet, embarrassed — he still thinks this is a prank.

### 12 · 23.0–27.0 s · F553–F648 · "HAR ROZ DEKHTE HO"
Two-shot: he is warm on the left, the bench is dark on the right. He is looking straight at an empty desk and it is
looking back.
- F553–F577 **read:** he stares at the bench; nothing there but a book.
- F577–F601 **read:** the page turns again, and the same sentence is written on it.
- F601–F648 **read:** *"Sir, aap mujhe har roz dekhte ho."* — sad, calm, patient. The camera does not move. There is no
  face to look at. That is the idea.

### 13 · 27.0–30.0 s · F649–F720 · THE RED PAGE
ECU on the register, everything behind it out of focus. A trembling hand flips the page. The record writes itself, in red,
letter by letter: **RAHUL MISHRA**, then **DEAD — 17 YEARS AGO.**
- F649–F673 **read:** his hand does not want to turn the page.
- F673–F700 **read:** the first line, written on. Then the second. The ink is wet.
- F700–F720 **read:** one drop of red falls off the page and lands. Hold. Then only breathing.

### 14 · 30.0–33.0 s · F721–F792 · "MAR CHUKE HO"
Close on his face: wet eyes, a hand that will not stay still.
- F721–F748 **read:** he understands, and does not want to say it.
- F748–F768 **read:** *"Tum… 17 saal pehle mar chuke ho."* The voice breaks on "mar".
- F768–F792 **read:** glasses off, rub, on (the third time), and the pen drops out of his hand, rolls, and stops.

### 15 · 33.0–36.0 s · F793–F864 · "PAR AAP BHI TOH…"
Close on the book again. New line: **"Haan sir… par aap bhi toh…"** — unfinished.
- F793–F816 **read:** the page turns; the answer is starting.
- F816–F840 **read:** *"Haan sir… par aap bhi toh…"* and it stops. He waits for the rest. There is no rest.
- F840–F852 **read:** the light fails twice (F843 off, F845 on, F847 off, F849 on).
- F852–F864 **hold**: 1 s of silence. The shadow on the bench shifts 1 mm at F858.

### 16 · 36.0–39.0 s · F865–F936 · DARKNESS
Wide. The light goes to dark amber — not black; the desks are still there. He is a silhouette holding the register, not
moving.
- F865–F912 **read:** the room getting darker around him, and him not leaving.
- F912–F936 **read:** the dead plant sways 1 mm; the class photo peels 2 mm more. The room is still alive. He is not.

### 17 · 39.0–42.0 s · F937–F1008 · THE DOOR
Close on the door, hallway light behind it.
- F937–F985 **read:** the door swings shut, slowly, and the warm rectangle of hall light narrows to a line.
- F985–F1000 **read:** the handle turns, one frame (F988). The latch: CLACK at F995.
- F1000–F1008 **read:** the hall light is gone. The room is closed.

### 18 · 42.0–45.0 s · F1009–F1080 · THE WHISPER
Medium, behind him, facing the bench. He is still standing where we left him.
- F1009–F1030 **read:** his shoulders are down. He is not going to turn around again.
- F1021–F1068 **read:** the whisper, from the centre of frame, from everywhere: *"Sir… aapko yaad nahi? Aaj aapki bhi
  17th death anniversary hai."*
- F1068–F1080 **read:** the register falls out of his hand and hits the floor; the pages fan open.

### 19 · 45.0–49.0 s · F1081–F1176 · THE PHOTOGRAPH
Slow push-in on the register on the floor. At F1128 a 2.35:1 letterbox slams in.
- F1081–F1128 **read:** the register is open on the floor, the pages fanned, and there is a photograph pasted inside:
  the teacher at twenty-five, gap-toothed, all his hair, the same cracked glasses.
- F1128–F1152 **read:** red, below the photo: **VERMA, RAVI** / **DEAD — 17 YEARS AGO.** The letterbox arrives with it.
  The red pulses on a two-frame heartbeat. A 40 Hz sub-bass starts, and a heartbeat at F1130 and F1150.
- F1152–F1176 **read:** a deep school gong, three seconds of decay. Hold on the photograph. At F1160 the eyes in the
  photograph shift 1 px.

### 20 · 49.0–50.0 s · F1177–F1200 · BLACK
Pure black `#0A0A0A`. No drawing at all.
- F1177 **sound:** a school bell, loud, warm and clear, one second. This is the only clean, happy sound in the film.
- F1189 **sound:** one child's giggle, half a second, cut off mid-laugh.
- F1200 **sound:** silence. End.

## Timing notes for anyone re-cutting this

- The film has **two silences** and they are doing the work: F353–F361 (the fan stops) and F852–F864 (the answer stops).
  Do not fill them.
- The **red** is the only red. Do not introduce another. Do not tint the photograph red; the photograph stays warm paper
  colour, and the red sits under it like a stamp.
- The **only two camera moves** are shot 2 (push onto the bench) and shot 19 (push onto the photograph). Everything else
  is locked off. If a locked shot feels slow, the answer is a read that hasn't landed, not a camera move.
- The film **never shows the boy**. Any cut that suggests a figure at the bench (a shadow, a silhouette, a hand) kills the
  ending: the point is that there is nothing there, and the teacher is the one who was never in the room.
